\title{\vspace{-2em}\bfseries Measuring Progress on Long-Tailed Dental Radiograph
Segmentation: Leakage, Ceilings, and Noise Floors}

\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We report a systematic negative result on a 31-class panoramic dental radiograph
corpus: across nineteen training configurations spanning boundary-aware
objectives, class-balanced losses, frequency-aware DETR training, an
architectural change to the mask head, and decoupled classifier retraining, no
configuration improves on a stock baseline beyond seed-level variation.

The contribution is not the null itself but the apparatus required to establish
it, each element of which caught a confident wrong answer that would otherwise
have been reported. \textbf{(1)} The corpus cannot be evaluated as distributed:
95.9\,\% of its test partition shares source images with training. \textbf{(2)}
Perceptual hashing alone is not a leakage test on this modality, with a
false-positive rate near 100\,\%. \textbf{(3)} The mask representation imposes a
measurable ceiling that is \emph{not} the binding constraint; a
higher-resolution head with 878{,}432 \emph{fewer} parameters performs
0.72\,pp worse, as predicted. \textbf{(4)} Distance metrics conditioned on
per-model subsets invert their own sign once compared on the intersection of
scored cases. \textbf{(5)} Group means over low-support classes fabricate
results: in one case 98\,\% of a $+0.53$\,pp mAP gain came from a single class
with \emph{two} test instances. \textbf{(6)} The published logit-adjustment
constant does not transfer from $\sim$100:1 to 34{,}320:1 imbalance.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Long-tailed instance segmentation and detection on clinical imagery is an active
area, and the standard toolkit---class-balanced losses, logit adjustment,
repeat-factor sampling, boundary-aware objectives---is well established on
natural-image benchmarks. We set out to apply that toolkit to a 31-class dental
radiograph corpus with 34{,}320:1 imbalance and measure the contribution of each
component.

None of it worked. That is worth reporting, because the reasons are specific,
measurable, and mostly not about the methods. The corpus as distributed cannot
support evaluation at all. The architecture imposes a ceiling that is real but
not binding. The metrics the field uses for contour quality invert their sign
under a denominator that differs per model. And group means over classes with
single-digit support will manufacture a headline on demand---twice, in our case,
before we caught them.

We regard contributions (2), (4) and (5) as the most transferable: each is a
measurement protocol that any group working on long-tailed clinical
segmentation can apply, and each caught an error in our own work that would have
survived review.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Long-tailed recognition.}
Class-balanced loss weighting via effective number of samples~\cite{cui2019classbalanced}
and logit adjustment~\cite{menon2021logit} are the standard remedies.
Kang~et~al.~\cite{kang2020decoupling} showed representation and classifier
learning should be \emph{decoupled}, with rebalancing applied at the classifier
stage; Wang~et~al.~\cite{wang2019calibration} adapt this to instance
segmentation. Repeat-factor sampling~\cite{gupta2019lvis} rebalances at the data
level, and Seesaw loss~\cite{wang2021seesaw} rescales gradients per class pair.

Our results bear on the \emph{regime} these were validated in.
Menon~et~al.~\cite{menon2021logit} tune $\tau=1.0$ on CIFAR-LT and ImageNet-LT
at roughly 100:1 to 1000:1 imbalance. At 34{,}320:1 the same constant applies a
$+11.47$ logit shift and destroys the classifier. We find the failure monotonic
in $\tau$, which localises the problem to magnitude rather than principle.

\paragraph{High-quality mask prediction.}
PointRend~\cite{kirillov2020pointrend} recasts segmentation as rendering,
refining adaptively at uncertain points. RefineMask~\cite{zhang2021refinemask}
fuses fine-grained features in stages. Mask
Transfiner~\cite{ke2022transfiner} represents masks as a quadtree and corrects
only error-prone nodes. All three exist because coarse mask prediction is a
known failure mode. We measure that failure mode directly rather than assuming
it, and find it is \emph{not} what limits our model---a result invisible without
the model-free ceiling measurement of Section~\ref{sec:ceiling}.

\paragraph{Boundary-aware objectives.}
Boundary loss~\cite{kervadec2018boundary} integrates the prediction against a
precomputed distance map; HD loss~\cite{karimi2019hausdorff} estimates Hausdorff
distance directly; Boundary IoU~\cite{cheng2021boundaryiou} is an evaluation
metric restricted to a contour band. Our band-Dice objective is a synthesis of
known components---morphological gradient as a differentiable edge extractor,
soft Dice~\cite{milletari2016vnet} as the overlap objective---and we say so
rather than claiming a new loss family.

\paragraph{Augmentation.}
Copy-paste~\cite{ghiasi2021copypaste} reliably helps on natural images. We find
it harmful here, and propose a mechanism: in panoramic radiographs anatomical
position is part of the class definition, so a finding pasted into an
anatomically impossible location teaches the model to detect the paste rather
than the pathology.

\subsection{Perceptual Hashing Alone Is Not a Leakage Test}

On natural images a dHash Hamming distance below ${\sim}5$ bits is strong
evidence of duplication. On panoramic radiographs it is nearly meaningless:
every image is the same anatomy, same framing, same grayscale statistics, so
unrelated studies routinely fall within 5 bits.

Run without pixel confirmation, dHash reports \textbf{792{,}811 leaking pairs on
a split we independently verify as clean}---a false-positive rate near 100\,\%.
Any audit of this modality that stops at perceptual hashing will condemn a clean
split, or worse, be tuned until it stops complaining.

Two-stage detection---dHash as a cheap candidate generator, normalised
cross-correlation as confirmation---separates the cases cleanly. Genuine
re-encoded duplicates score $\mathrm{NCC}\ge0.99$; distinct studies stay below
0.94.

\subsection{Statistical Power}

Sixteen of 31 classes appear in fewer than 10 validation images; two appear in
none; eleven tail classes score exactly zero. \textbf{Image counts, not instance
counts, are the effective sample size.} No long-tail claim is supportable on
this corpus, and we make none.

\section{The Representation Ceiling, and Why It Is Not the Constraint}
\label{sec:ceiling}

YOLOv8-seg predicts 32 coefficients per instance and reconstructs masks as a
linear combination of prototypes at input$/4$---$160\times160$ at
$\mathrm{imgsz}=640$. The median instance here is \textbf{6\,px on that grid};
68\,\% are under 8\,px.

We bound what any model could achieve by round-tripping every ground-truth mask
through the grid and scoring it against itself.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
prototype grid & mean Dice & cannot reach IoU 0.75 \\
\midrule
\textbf{$160\times160$ (stock)} & \textbf{0.8963} & \textbf{17.7\,\%} \\
$320\times320$ & 0.9492 & 5.4\,\% \\
$640\times640$ & 0.9901 & 0.5\,\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Model-free ceiling over 20{,}601 instances. Root canal treatment is
capped at IoU 0.622 and caries at 0.721---both \emph{below} the 0.75
threshold, excluding 4{,}493 instances from AP75 before training begins.}
\end{table}

The model's behaviour agrees: mask AP50 reaches 87\,\% of box AP50, mask AP75
only 49\,\%.

\subsection{The Inference We Initially Drew Was Wrong}

This looks like a diagnosis. It is not.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\toprule
ceiling at the current grid & 0.8963 \\
\textbf{achieved by the model} & \textbf{0.6969} \\
headroom already available & 0.1994 \\
additional headroom from doubling & 0.0529 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{The model sits at 78\,\% of the ceiling it already has.}
\end{table}

We tested it. A prototype head fed from P2 (stride 4) rather than P3 (stride 8)
yields $320\times320$ prototypes from genuine high-resolution features, using
sub-pixel convolution~\cite{shi2016subpixel} to keep the wide tensor off the
high-resolution grid, with \textbf{878{,}432 fewer parameters} than stock so any
gain could not be a capacity artifact. Against a matched control at identical
budget and protocol: \textbf{$-0.72$\,pp mAP, $-0.49$\,pp AP75}.

The ceiling is real and worth reporting. It is not the binding constraint, and
the check that establishes this---comparing achieved performance against the
ceiling---costs one line of arithmetic. We ran it after the experiment. It
should be run before.

\section{Two Metric Failures That Produce Confident Wrong Answers}

\subsection{Distance Metrics on Per-Model Subsets}

HD95 and ASSD are undefined when either mask is empty, so implementations
average them over cases where both are non-empty. \textbf{That denominator
differs per model.} A model that misses a hard structure drops that case from
its own average and can post better distances by predicting less.

Averaged per model, our boundary objective appeared \textbf{17.3\,\% better on
HD95 and 24.6\,\% better on ASSD, with disjoint confidence intervals}---the
strongest result in the project.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrrc}
\toprule
metric & ref. & bnd. & difference & sep. \\
\midrule
Dice & 0.6953 & 0.6969 & $+0.0017$ & no \\
IoU & 0.5685 & 0.5706 & $+0.0021$ & no \\
boundary F & 0.7857 & 0.7881 & $+0.0024$ & no \\
\textbf{HD95} & 66.36 & 68.66 & $\mathbf{+2.30}$ \textbf{(worse)} & \textbf{yes} \\
ASSD & 18.40 & 18.82 & $+0.43$ (worse) & no \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Recomputed on the 5{,}385 cases both models scored, paired, with the
per-case difference bootstrapped over images. Correcting the denominator did
not attenuate the effect---it \emph{inverted the sign}.}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Protocol.} Any metric conditioned on a per-model subset must be
compared on the intersection of cases both models scored, paired, with coverage
reported alongside. Reporting distances without coverage permits a model to win
by predicting less.

\subsection{Group Means over Low-Support Classes}

Two headline gains, in two independent projects, dissolved under decomposition.
For detection, decoupled classifier retraining gave $+0.53$\,pp mAP and
$+1.25$\,pp tail---positive on every metric, with the largest gain exactly where
a long-tail method should deliver.

\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\toprule

\textbf{share of total} & \textbf{98\,\%} \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\paragraph{Protocol.} Report per-class deltas with support counts alongside any
group mean, and state the result restricted to classes with adequate support. A
group AP over classes with single-digit support is not a measurement.

inconsistently diverged with \texttt{inf} or \texttt{NaN} cost matrices in the
Hungarian matcher; every consistent arm was stable. Consistency prevents
divergence without producing accuracy.



\section{The Mask Deficit Is Coefficient Prediction, Not Representation}

The 20-point gap between what the model achieves (Dice 0.6969) and what its
prototype grid allows (0.8963) had no attribution. Prototype-based heads
reconstruct each mask as $\sigma(\mathbf{c}^\top P)$, so either the learned
32-dimensional basis cannot represent these shapes, or the basis is adequate and
the coefficient head fails to locate the right point in it. Those call for
opposite fixes.

We separate them without training. For every ground-truth instance we solve, in
closed form, for the coefficient vector that best reconstructs it from the
model's own prototypes,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{c}^{*} = \arg\min_{\mathbf{c}} \; \lVert P^{\top}\mathbf{c} - y \rVert^{2},
\end{equation}
over the instance's box crop, where $y\in\{-1,+1\}$ is the ground-truth mask.
Thresholding $P^{\top}\mathbf{c}^{*}$ gives the best mask \emph{any} coefficient
head could produce from that basis.

This closes the loop on every negative result above. Boundary terms and
comparator losses reshape the \emph{objective}; higher prototype resolution
enlarges the \emph{representation}. Neither is the binding constraint, which is
why neither moved the number, and why the P2-fed head performed worse rather
than better---it added resolution to a basis that was never the limit.

The head capacities make this concrete. In YOLOv8x-seg the coefficient branch
bottlenecks 320 to 80 channels and carries 1.33\,M parameters, against 2.26\,M
for the prototype generator and 7.41\,M for classification. The smallest of the
three heads is responsible for 85\,\% of the mask deficit.

\section{Noise Floor (Measured)}

The reference configuration was trained at three seeds, everything else
identical. Determinism is exact on this pipeline, so seed choice is provably the
only source of variation.

Three consequences, and they are not the same verdict. The seven-arm ablation
spans a 0.36\,pp range, \emph{inside} the band---selecting a winner on mAP was
selecting noise. The best segmentation arm on test is $+0.21$\,pp, sitting
\emph{exactly at} the threshold. But the isolated boundary effect on AP75 is
$+0.70$\,pp against a $\pm0.50$\,pp floor, so it \textbf{exceeds} noise on
validation at 50 epochs, and then reverses on test at 100 epochs. That is a
\textbf{transfer failure, not a noise result}: the objective does what its
mechanism predicts under the conditions it was measured in, and that does not
survive a change of split and schedule.

\section{Discussion}

The standard long-tail and boundary-aware toolkit does not transfer to this
corpus, and most of the reasons are not about the methods. Two are about the
data: it cannot be evaluated as shipped, and 16 of 31 classes lack the support
to measure anything. Two are about measurement: distance metrics and group means
both produce confident wrong answers under conditions easy to satisfy
accidentally. One is about regime: a published constant validated at 100:1 does
not survive 34{,}320:1.

Only one is about a method---the band-Dice objective produces the AP75/AP50
signature its mechanism predicts, and that signal does not survive to test.

We think the protocols of Sections~3.1, 5.1 and 5.2 are the durable output. Each
is cheap, each applies to any long-tailed clinical segmentation study, and each
caught an error in our own work that would otherwise have been published.

\section{Limitations}

Single seed on all reported arms; the three-seed noise floor is measured above. Single corpus, single backbone family per task. No comparison
against prior published results, because none exist for this dataset. No
external benchmark, and therefore no long-tail claim in the title, abstract or
conclusions. Two detection cells are recorded as diverged rather than scored.
