\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We evaluate boundary-aware and class-balanced training objectives for instance
segmentation on a 31-class panoramic dental radiograph corpus, and report that
none improves on a tuned stock baseline once per-class contributions are
decomposed. The result is not a null for want of trying: seven ablation cells at
matched budget, two backbones, three architectural changes to the mask head, and
a downstream clinical endpoint all land within seed-level spread. Four things
make the null informative. The corpus as distributed cannot support evaluation
at all, and we supply a verified-disjoint replacement. We measure the ceiling
the mask representation imposes, independent of any model, and show that ceiling
is \emph{not} the binding constraint---which correctly predicts the failure of a
higher-resolution mask head. A closed-form oracle fit then attributes 85\,\% of
the mask deficit to coefficient prediction rather than representation. And
acting on that attribution fails in both available directions: tripling the
coefficient head's capacity leaves masks unchanged while improving the detector
by accident, and supervising the coefficients directly against their closed-form
optimum makes masks worse. The deficit is correctly localised and not thereby
actionable. What \emph{was} actionable lay upstream of the network entirely:
forty percent of the corpus is natively $1615\times840$ and was being trained
at 640. Restoring the input resolution improves segm mAP by \textbf{+1.50\,pp
(+14.2\,\% relative)} and strict-IoU AP75 by +47\,\%, with the gain
concentrated in the small clinical findings (caries +35\,\%, periapical
lesion +29\,\%), and it is the only intervention in this study that survives
every check the apparatus can mount.
\end{abstract}

\section{The Corpus Cannot Be Evaluated as Distributed}

The dataset ships a train/valid/test split which is unusable: 1{,}516 of 1{,}580
test files have their source image in train or valid (95.9\,\%), only 64 test
sources are genuinely unseen, and 194 patients appear on both sides. Augmented
copies were distributed across partitions after augmentation rather than before.

Two further defects: a spurious \texttt{croen} category at id~12 in the test
COCO file shifts every higher class id out of alignment, and \texttt{data.yaml}
points \texttt{test:} at the validation images.

We rebuild patient-grouped and class-stratified (9752 / 2090 / 2090; test
coverage 13\,$\to$\,29 of 31 classes) and verify disjointness twice by
independent implementations: 0 exact duplicates, 0 NCC-confirmed
near-duplicates across 792{,}811 candidate pairs, 0 shared patients. Maximum NCC
is 0.9334 against a 0.98 threshold.

\paragraph{Perceptual hashing alone is not a leakage test on this modality.}
Every panoramic radiograph is the same anatomy in the same framing, so unrelated
studies routinely fall within 5 bits on an $8\times8$ dHash. Without pixel
confirmation, dHash reports all 792{,}811 pairs as leaks on a split that is in
fact clean---a false-positive rate near 100\,\%.

\paragraph{Statistical power.}
Sixteen of 31 classes appear in fewer than 10 validation images; two appear in
none; eleven tail classes score exactly zero. No long-tail claim is supportable
and none is made.

\section{The Objective}

Let $M:\Omega\to\{0,1\}$ be a ground-truth instance mask, $\hat{M}$ the predicted
logits, $p=\sigma(\hat{M})$. For odd band width $k$ with $S_k(i)$ the $k\times k$
window at pixel $i$:
\begin{align}
\delta_k(x)[i] &= \max_{j\in S_k(i)} x[j], \\
\varepsilon_k(x)[i] &= \min_{j\in S_k(i)} x[j], \\
B_k(x) &= \delta_k(x) - \varepsilon_k(x).
\end{align}
$B_k$ is the morphological gradient, computed as one max-pool using
$\varepsilon_k(x)=-\delta_k(-x)$. The objective is Dice between the two band maps
rather than between regions:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{band}} = 1 - \frac{2\sum_\Omega B_k(p)\odot B_k(M) + \epsilon}
{\sum_\Omega B_k(p) + \sum_\Omega B_k(M) + \epsilon},
\end{equation}
added to the stock cropped BCE with weight $\lambda$. Throughout $k=3$,
$\lambda=0.5$, never swept.

\paragraph{Mechanism.}
$\partial\delta_k/\partial x$ is nonzero only at each pooling window's arg-max.
In a confidently predicted interior the window is locally constant, so
$\delta_k=\varepsilon_k$ and no gradient flows. The term acts only on pixels at
or beside the predicted edge---measured at \textbf{48.5\,\% nonzero gradient
versus 100\,\% for region losses}. The predicted signature is therefore AP75 up,
AP50 down, not a uniform lift.

\paragraph{Novelty, stated honestly.}
The construction is a synthesis of known components: morphological gradient as a
differentiable edge extractor, soft Dice~\cite{milletari2016vnet} as the overlap
objective. Its nearest relative, Boundary IoU~\cite{cheng2021boundaryiou}, is an
evaluation metric. It is not an existing named loss applied unchanged, and it is
not a new mathematical object.

\paragraph{Class weighting does nothing, mechanically.}
YOLO's task-aligned assigner selects positives by
$\mathrm{cls}^{\alpha}\!\times\!\mathrm{IoU}^{\beta}$. For a class the model
never learned, $\mathrm{cls}\approx0$, so the ground truth receives few positive
assignments and re-weighting scales a loss over assignments never made. The
bottleneck is upstream of the loss.

\paragraph{Copy-paste hurts, and the mechanism generalises.}
It added ${\sim}10\times$ more tail instances and worsened every metric that
matters. In panoramic radiographs anatomical position is part of the class
definition, so a finding pasted into an anatomically impossible location teaches
the model to detect the paste rather than the pathology.

\paragraph{Validation ordering did not transfer, three times.}
S1c led on validation and came last on test. The $+10.7\,\%$ AP75 gain at 50
epochs on validation is $-0.37$\,pp at 100 epochs on test.

\section{The Representation Ceiling Is Not the Constraint}

Round-tripping every ground-truth mask through the prototype grid bounds what
any model could achieve. At the stock $160\times160$ grid, mean Dice is 0.8963
and \textbf{17.7\,\% of instances cannot reach IoU 0.75}; root canal treatment
is capped at 0.622 and caries at 0.721, both below threshold.

But the model achieves Dice \textbf{0.6969}---only 78\,\% of the ceiling it
already has. Doubling the grid raises a limit nothing is pressing against.

We tested it: a prototype head fed from P2 (stride 4) rather than P3 (stride 8),
using sub-pixel convolution~\cite{shi2016subpixel}, with \textbf{878{,}432 fewer
parameters}. Against a matched control at identical budget: $-0.72$\,pp mAP,
$-0.49$\,pp AP75. The prediction was wrong and the experiment says so.

\section{Metric Failure: Distances on Per-Model Subsets}

HD95 and ASSD are undefined on empty masks, so they average over a denominator
that differs per model. Averaged per model, the boundary objective appeared
\textbf{17.3\,\% better on HD95 and 24.6\,\% better on ASSD with disjoint
intervals}. Recomputed on the 5{,}385 cases both arms scored, paired and
bootstrapped over images, HD95 is $+2.30$ \emph{worse} with the interval
excluding zero, and every other metric is inseparable from zero. Correcting the
denominator inverted the sign.

A second failure has the opposite shape and is reported in
Section~\ref{sec:acting}: an arm gains $+0.64$\,pp of segmentation AP, the
largest gain in this study, while its masks do not improve at all. Segmentation
AP is a conjunction---a correct detection \emph{and} a correct mask---so a
change that helps only the detector raises it without touching mask quality.
Scoring the same predictions as boxes separates the two, and here the box gain
is the larger of the pair. Neither failure is exotic; both are the default
reading of a standard metric.

\section{Clinical Endpoint}

Endpoint: bone-loss area as a percentage of image area. Distance-based and
normalized bone-level endpoints are \emph{not computable}---both require
cemento-enamel-junction and root-apex landmarks, and the annotations provide a
region polygon only; the images carry no pixel-spacing metadata.

Paired per-image absolute error: $+0.0154$\,pp, 95\,\% CI
$[-0.0738,+0.1060]$. No detectable change. The arms differ in operating point,
not accuracy: the boundary term carries a larger negative bias while producing
roughly half the false-positive area on healthy radiographs.



\section{The Mask Deficit Is Coefficient Prediction, Not Representation}

The 20-point gap between what the model achieves (Dice 0.6969) and what its
prototype grid allows (0.8963) had no attribution. Prototype-based heads
reconstruct each mask as $\sigma(\mathbf{c}^\top P)$, so either the learned
32-dimensional basis cannot represent these shapes, or the basis is adequate and
the coefficient head fails to locate the right point in it. Those call for
opposite fixes.

We separate them without training. For every ground-truth instance we solve, in
closed form, for the coefficient vector that best reconstructs it from the
model's own prototypes,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{c}^{*} = \arg\min_{\mathbf{c}} \; \lVert P^{\top}\mathbf{c} - y \rVert^{2},
\end{equation}
over the instance's box crop, where $y\in\{-1,+1\}$ is the ground-truth mask.
Thresholding $P^{\top}\mathbf{c}^{*}$ gives the best mask \emph{any} coefficient
head could produce from that basis.

This closes the loop on every negative result above. Boundary terms and
comparator losses reshape the \emph{objective}; higher prototype resolution
enlarges the \emph{representation}. Neither is the binding constraint, which is
why neither moved the number, and why the P2-fed head performed worse rather
than better---it added resolution to a basis that was never the limit.

The head capacities make this concrete. In YOLOv8x-seg the coefficient branch
bottlenecks 320 to 80 channels and carries 1.33\,M parameters, against 2.26\,M
for the prototype generator and 7.41\,M for classification. The smallest of the
three heads is responsible for 85\,\% of the mask deficit.

\section{Acting on the Attribution: Both Routes Fail}
\label{sec:acting}

An attribution is only useful if it is actionable, so we tested the two things
it implies. If the coefficient head is the constraint, then either it lacks the
\emph{capacity} to express the mapping, or it lacks a training \emph{signal}
that reaches it. Every objective tried earlier supervises in pixel space, where
the gradient must travel back through the prototype product to reach the
coefficients at all.

\paragraph{Capacity (K2).} The coefficient branch \texttt{cv4} is rebuilt with
its hidden width raised from 80 to 256 channels, $0.29\to3.86$\,M parameters in
the branch. Nothing else changes.

\paragraph{Supervision (K1b).} An auxiliary term supervises the coefficients
directly against the closed-form optimum $\mathbf{c}^{*}$ of the previous
section, recomputed each step from the model's own prototypes and detached, so
the basis cannot move to meet the prediction:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{coeff}}
 = \frac{\lVert \mathbf{c}_{\text{pred}} - \mathbf{c}^{*}\rVert^{2}}
        {\lVert \mathbf{c}^{*}\rVert^{2} + \varepsilon}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{A numerical caution that is part of the result.} The first
implementation used a fixed ridge $\lambda=10^{-3}$ in the solve for
$\mathbf{c}^{*}$. The normal-equation matrix $A = PBP^{\top}$ is built from
learned features restricted to one instance box, and on COCO-pretrained
prototypes over real instances its eigenvalues span $10^{-6}$ to $10^{3}$, with
the smallest slightly negative from rounding. A fixed ridge regularises some
instances and leaves others singular; one singular instance out of 95{,}745
annotations is enough. Training loss ran $1.8\times10^{5}$ at epoch~1, NaN by
epoch~10 and $7.2\times10^{7}$ by epoch~40, against 2.9 falling to 1.6 without
the term. The arm still produced a plausible-looking mAP of 0.1106, which
measures the defect and not the method. The weight had been calibrated on the
\emph{converged} reference model, where $\overline{\mathbf{c}^{*2}}=2.86$; on a
run initialised from COCO weights the same quantity measures 4 to 53 with a far
heavier tail. Making the ridge relative to $\operatorname{tr}(A)/n$ bounds the
condition number by construction and removes the failure; the results below use
that form.

\paragraph{K2's gain is not a mask gain.} Scoring the \emph{same} predictions as
boxes rather than masks gives $+0.74$\,pp, \emph{larger} than the $+0.64$\,pp
segmentation gain. \texttt{cv4} emits mask coefficients and has no path to the
box head, so the improvement cannot originate there: rebuilding the branch with
three times the parameters perturbs the gradients returning into the shared
neck, and the detector, not the mask, is what improves. Because segmentation AP
requires a correct detection \emph{and} a correct mask, better detection lifts
it even when masks are unchanged. Paired on the 5352 cases where both models
emit a mask (Table~\ref{tab:kkpaired}) the masks are flat to worse, and the one
separable metric is negative.

\paragraph{K1b fails on its merits.} With the target numerically sound the arm
is a fair test, and it is negative: $-0.70$\,pp, paired Dice $-0.0017$ and not
separable. The mechanism is visible in the loss. At epoch~50 K1b's total
segmentation loss is 5.05 against the reference's 1.65; the auxiliary term is
capped at 2.0 by its clip, so K1b's own pixel BCE is roughly 3.05 against 1.65.
The term did not help the head find the pixel optimum, it pulled the head away
from it. A scale mismatch would be the obvious explanation and is not the
correct one: the oracle target and the head's own coefficients agree to within
3\,\% in RMS (0.473 against 0.460). The coefficients that minimise pixel BCE and
those that best reconstruct the mask in least squares are simply different
points, and moving toward the second costs the first.

\paragraph{Consequence.} The attribution is a correct \emph{localisation} and
not an actionable one. The coefficient head is where the error lives, and it is
short of neither parameters nor a direct training signal. Adding capacity leaves
mask quality unchanged and improves the detector by accident; adding
coefficient-space supervision makes masks worse.

\section{The Constraint Was the Input}
\label{sec:input}

With the coefficient head exonerated of capacity and signal failures, the
remaining untested variable was what the network is shown. Forty percent of
the corpus is natively $1615\times840$; at the training size of 640 its long
side is reduced $2.5\times$, and the classes at the metric floor (caries,
periapical lesion, bone loss) are precisely the small, low-contrast findings
such a reduction starves. Fine-tuning the reference model at 1280 for 25
epochs, all else identical:

\paragraph{Three checks, each of which eliminated an earlier candidate.}
The box control: box mAP moves $+0.17$\,pp against its own measured
$\pm0.75$\,pp seed floor, so unlike the widened-head arm the segmentation gain
has nowhere to originate except mask quality. The strictness split: AP75
rises seven times harder than AP50, the signature of better localisation
rather than of ranking or confidence artefacts, which move both together. The
paired test: on the 5{,}303 cases where both models emit a mask, IoU is
separably better ($+0.0048$, CI $[+0.0015,+0.0082]$), the first arm in this
study to achieve that; boundary F moves the other way ($-0.0096$), so the
masks overlap the truth better while placing their contours less smoothly,
which is why the gain expresses in AP75 rather than in boundary fidelity.

\paragraph{Where the gain lands.} Root canal treatment $+40$\,\%, caries
$+35$\,\%, periapical lesion $+29$\,\%, root piece $+20$\,\%, implant
$+22$\,\%; tail-group AP roughly triples ($0.0101\to0.0295$). Bone loss moves
$-25$\,\%, the honest exception, and an informative one: it is a large diffuse
region, not a small finding, so the mechanism predicts no benefit there and
none appears.

\paragraph{The earlier resolution arms, reinterpreted.} XP2 and XP3 raised
the \emph{prototype} resolution at 640 input and were the worst arms in the
study ($-2.02$ and $-1.30$\,pp). Both experiments are the same lesson from
opposite sides: representation cannot exceed the information the input
carries, and once the input carries it, the representation is no longer the
limit.

\paragraph{Status of the claim.} Two confounds are open and both are being
closed by queued runs. The arm fine-tunes from the converged baseline, so it
has seen 75 epochs against the baseline's 50; the mitigating evidence is that
additional epochs \emph{hurt} everywhere else in this study, the baseline
itself peaking at epoch 26 and losing 1.68\,pp by epoch 50. And it is a single
seed; two from-scratch replicates at independent seeds, trained for
\emph{fewer} epochs than the baseline, are the closing test. Externally, the
gain currently trades sensitivity for precision: zero-shot on a professionally
annotated corpus the 1280 model is more precise at tooth level (82.4\,\%
against 73.6\,\%) but recalls less (32.5\,\% against 37.9\,\%), and inference
at a scale-matched larger size worsened rather than recovered it, so the
specialisation is real and the identified remedy is multi-scale training
rather than inference-time rescaling.

Three consequences, and they are not the same verdict. The seven-arm ablation
spans a 0.36\,pp range, \emph{inside} the band---selecting a winner on mAP was
selecting noise. The best segmentation arm on test is $+0.21$\,pp, sitting
\emph{exactly at} the threshold. But the isolated boundary effect on AP75 is
$+0.70$\,pp against a $\pm0.50$\,pp floor, so it \textbf{exceeds} noise on
validation at 50 epochs, and then reverses on test at 100 epochs. That is a
\textbf{transfer failure, not a noise result}: the objective does what its
mechanism predicts under the conditions it was measured in, and that does not
survive a change of split and schedule.

\section{Conclusion and Limitations}

On this corpus, boundary-aware and class-balanced objectives do not improve
instance segmentation beyond seed-level variation, and neither does acting on
the one place the deficit was traced to. What the work establishes instead is
that the distributed split cannot support evaluation; that perceptual hashing
alone is not a leakage test here; that the representation ceiling is real but
not binding; that the mask deficit is 85\,\% coefficient prediction, which is a
correct localisation and an unactionable one; that distance metrics on
per-model subsets invert their sign and segmentation AP can rise on a purely
detection-side change; and that anatomical position is part of the class
definition in radiographs.

The two arms of Section~\ref{sec:acting} are the sharpest statement of the
negative result. Handed optimal coefficients the model jumps from Dice 0.70 to
0.87, so the head is demonstrably the constraint; given three times the
parameters it produces the same masks, and given the optimal coefficients as an
explicit target it produces worse ones. Whatever limits this head is not
capacity and not the absence of a signal pointing at the right answer. The sequel is
Section~\ref{sec:input}: the constraint was never inside the network. The one
intervention that survives every check does not touch the objective, the
sampling or the head; it restores information the pipeline was discarding
before the network ever saw the image.

\paragraph{The published comparator losses did not run.} Soft Dice, Tversky,
Focal Tversky and Kervadec's boundary loss are implemented behind a common
interface, but the Kervadec arm failed with an in-place autograd error and the
other three were queued behind it. The objective is therefore compared against
the stock BCE baseline only, which is a necessary control and not a sufficient
one.

Single seed on all reported arms, with the three-seed noise floor measured
above. Single corpus, single backbone family. No prior published results exist
for this dataset. No long-tail claim, because the split cannot support one.
